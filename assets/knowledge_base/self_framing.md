# Self Framing — [CANDIDATE_NAME]

## How to generate this file

Run `/setup framing` to have Claude walk you through a Q&A about your honest strengths and gaps. This is the most important file for ensuring the pipeline doesn't over-claim or under-sell.

**Why this matters**: Without honest self-framing, the pipeline will either:
- Fabricate confidence in skills you don't have (gets caught in technical interviews)
- Undersell skills you do have (wastes good applications)

The goal is calibrated honesty — knowing exactly what you can claim and how.

---

## What this file should contain (after running /setup framing)

### ML/DS Experience Level
- How many years of hands-on ML experience (not just data work)?
- Most complex model you've shipped: algorithm, scale, production status?
- What's your honest assessment of your modeling depth vs. breadth?

### Technical Skill Honest Assessment
For each of your main skills, document the honest level:

**Example format:**
- **Python**: Daily driver for 5+ years. Comfortable with production code, not just notebooks.
- **SQL**: Advanced. BigQuery specialist. Can write complex window functions and CTEs.
- **[Skill you're learning]**: Actively building. Can follow tutorials and implement basics, but would not claim expertise. Do not present as proficient in CV or cover letter.

### Gaps and Learning in Progress
List skills in your profile that you're still building:
- What is the honest description of your current level?
- How should these be framed if they appear in a job requirement? (e.g., "actively building experience with X as a natural extension of Y")

### Domain Experience (honest)
- Which industries do you have DOCUMENTED work experience in? (list only real projects)
- Which industries have you studied or are familiar with but haven't worked in directly?
- Rule: never claim industry experience you don't have a project to back up.

### Production Deployment Experience
- Have you deployed models to production? (yes/no/partial)
- If yes: describe the setup (scheduled batch, real-time API, etc.) and scale
- If no/partial: what's the honest description?

### Years of Experience Claim
- What's the exact phrasing to use consistently? (e.g., "5+ years", "over 6 years")
- From what point do you count? Why?
- This must be consistent across all CVs and cover letters.

### Academic Credentials
- What's the exact name of your highest degree? (never abbreviate or paraphrase)
- Is it a well-known degree type? If not, how do you describe it in context?

### What NOT to claim
List specific things that should never appear in your CV or cover letters:
- Skills you have on your LinkedIn but haven't actually used professionally
- Outcomes or metrics you cannot verify
- Projects where your contribution was minimal (< 20%)
- Anything you'd struggle to explain in a 5-minute technical conversation
